# Chapter 11: Introduction to Bug Bounty Hunting

> **Learning objectives.** Explain what bug bounty programs and VDPs are; distinguish public/private programs and disclosure models; describe economics, severity, CVSS, CWE, CVE, triage, duplicates, and reputation; and communicate professionally.

## 11.1 What Bug Bounty Programs Are

A **bug bounty program** is an arrangement where an organization invites external researchers to find and report vulnerabilities in defined assets, offering recognition and/or rewards for valid findings. A **Vulnerability Disclosure Program (VDP)** typically accepts reports without monetary rewards. Both formalize the previously ad-hoc practice of contacting a vendor about a security issue.

Programs define:

- **Scope:** which assets and vulnerability types are in/out.
- **Rules of engagement:** what testing is allowed.
- **Submission process:** where and how to report.
- **Triage and rewards:** how findings are evaluated and compensated.

## 11.2 Public vs. Private Programs

- **Public programs:** anyone can register and submit. Often have broad scopes and high competition; duplicate rate is high.
- **Private programs:** invitation-only, limited researcher pools; less noise, often higher value findings.
- **Time-boxed events**: temporary programs with specific goals.

**Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure (CVD)** is the structured process of reporting, validating, fixing, and disclosing. ISO/IEC 29147 (disclosure) and ISO/IEC 30111 (handling) are the international references. Responsible disclosure means giving the vendor a reasonable remediation window before public discussion; many programs specify a disclosure timeline and require confidentiality until fixed.

## 11.3 Economics and Rewards

Rewards vary enormously by program, severity, asset criticality, and the organization's budget. Never rely on remembered figures — they change. Consult each program's published policy on the platform. Factors influencing reward:

- Severity and exploitability.
- Sensitivity of affected data.
- Quality of the report and PoC.
- Novelty and research effort.
- Whether the issue was previously unknown.

**Important:** income from bug bounty is highly variable and uncertain. Most researchers earn nothing in early months; many never earn significant amounts. Treat it as a skill-building activity with *possible* upside, not a reliable income source. This book makes no promise of earnings, rewards, certifications, or employment.

## 11.4 Severity, CVSS, CWE, CVE

- **CVSS (Common Vulnerability Scoring System):** an open standard (currently CVSS v4.0) producing a 0--10 score from base, temporal, and environmental metrics. Learn to compute base scores, but remember programs often use their own business-context severity.
- **CWE (Common Weakness Enumeration):** a taxonomy of software weakness types (e.g., CWE-79 XSS, CWE-89 SQLi, CWE-639 IDOR). MITRE maintains it.
- **CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures):** an identifier for a *specific* published vulnerability (e.g., CVE-2021-44228). Program findings in web apps usually do not receive CVEs.
- **Security advisory:** a vendor publication describing a vulnerability, affected versions, and fixes.

Severity classification typically considers impact (confidentiality/integrity/availability), attack vector, complexity, privileges required, user interaction, and scope. A self-XSS that affects only the attacker is usually Informative; stored XSS affecting all users can be High.

## 11.5 Triage, Duplicates, and Outcomes

Typical report states:

- **New / Triage:** awaiting initial review.
- **Valid / Accepted:** confirmed; bounty decision pending.
- **Duplicate:** the same issue was already reported; usually no reward.
- **Informative / Not applicable / Not a vulnerability:** no security impact or out of scope.
- **Out of scope:** asset or type not covered.
- **Resolved / Fixed:** remediation deployed; retest may be allowed.
- **Disclosed:** public after coordination.

**Common rejection reasons:** out of scope, duplicate, no security impact (e.g., version banner disclosure), self-only issues, requires unlikely preconditions, theoretical without PoC, caused by the researcher's own actions (e.g., self-DoS), or violates program rules.

Professional response to rejection: read the triage comment, ask a concise clarifying question if needed, and move on. Arguing without new evidence damages reputation.

## 11.6 Researcher Reputation

Platforms track signal: valid report rate, duplication rate, clarity, and responsiveness. A strong reputation leads to private invitations and faster triage. Build it by submitting only well-validated, in-scope, clearly written reports and by treating triage staff professionally.

## 11.7 Professional Communication

- Use clear, concise language; avoid exaggeration.
- State preconditions and limitations honestly.
- Provide reproducible steps and evidence.
- Respect disclosure timelines and confidentiality.
- Never threaten, demand, or publicize prematurely.

## 11.8 Key Takeaways

- Bug bounty is a formalized, scoped research relationship — not permission to test anything.
- CVSS, CWE, and CVE describe different things; use them precisely.
- Duplicates and informative reports are normal; reputation comes from quality.
- Income is uncertain; prioritize learning and professionalism.
