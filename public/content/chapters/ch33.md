## 33.7 How to Read a Security Paper

1. **Abstract and introduction:** what problem, why it matters.
2. **Threat model:** what assumptions about attacker and defender.
3. **Design/mechanism:** how the attack or defense works.
4. **Evaluation:** datasets, baselines, and metrics — are claims supported?
5. **Limitations:** what the authors concede.
6. **Reproducibility:** is there code or data?
7. **Connections:** how does it relate to other work and to your own research?

## 33.8 Key Takeaways

- Foundational papers explain principles that still govern modern systems.
- AI security research is moving quickly; pair papers with current OWASP/NIST guidance.
- Verify citations and prefer official sources; do not cite from memory alone.
